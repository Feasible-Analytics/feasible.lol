Cloudmanic Labs has been building and shipping software for more than twenty years.
It's run by **Spicer Matthews**, who writes code, answers the support email, and signs
the invoices. That's not a growth-stage anecdote. It's the org chart.

We build two things. **[Harbor](https://harbor.my)** is a notes app — private,
offline-first, with your notes stored in a format you can take with you. **Feasible**
is website analytics that fits on one page and costs $9.99 a month. Both come from
the same habit: find a category where the price stopped tracking the cost, build the
version we wanted to buy, and charge one honest number for it.

The company is at 901 Brutscher Street, D112, Newberg, Oregon — a town of about
25,000 people forty minutes southwest of Portland. There's no office in San
Francisco, because there's no need for one.

## How we make money

People pay us $9.99 a month, or $99 a year. That's the entire business model.

There's no venture money to pay back, no ad product, no data broker on the other end
of the pipe. We don't sell what we collect, we don't share it, and we don't train
anything on it. The reason we can promise that isn't virtue — it's that the
subscription is the only revenue line there is, so we'd have to invent a second
business to betray the first one.

It also means [the price can be what it is](/why-feasible/). One binary, one SQLite
file per account, no cluster to feed. When the thing you sell is cheap to run, you
don't need a pricing ladder to make the numbers work.

## What "small" actually buys you

If you're weighing us against a competitor with a hundred employees and a funding
round, here's the trade, both directions.

**What you get.** The person who answers your email can change the code that same
afternoon. There's no tier-one script, no ticket routing, no "I've escalated this to
the product team." A bug report that's clear enough usually gets a reply with a fix
or a reason, not an acknowledgement. Feature requests land in front of someone who
can act on them. And nobody here has a quota that's met by moving a feature you use
into a higher tier.

**What you don't.** We're small, and there are real costs to that. There's no
24-hour phone line. There's no account manager, no dedicated Slack channel, no
quarterly business review. We don't have a SOC 2 report, an ISO 27001 certificate or
a third-party penetration test — [we say exactly what we do and don't have on the
security page](/security/). If your procurement process requires any of those, we
won't pass it, and we'd rather you learned that here than six weeks into an
evaluation.

Support runs on US Pacific time, from a person who sleeps. Most email gets a real
answer in a day or two. [Here's what to expect and how to make it faster](/support/).

## The obvious question

*What happens to my data if you disappear?*

It's the right question to ask a small company, and it's why two things are true from
day one. Feasible is [open source under AGPL-3.0-or-later](/open-source-web-analytics/) — the whole
product, the same build we run, not a cut-down community edition — so the software
outlives the company. And your data exports as plain CSV, raw events included,
whenever you ask, in every state your account can be in, including after you've
stopped paying.

We'd rather compete on being worth staying with than on being expensive to leave.
