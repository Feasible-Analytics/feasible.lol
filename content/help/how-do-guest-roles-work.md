---
title: "How do guest roles work?"
description: "A guest gets one site and no idea the others exist. Two flavours — view only, or view and edit its settings."
category: "account-and-billing"
weight: 70
---

A guest is attached to one site, not to your [team](/help/what-are-the-team-roles/).

They sign in, they see that site, and there's no trace of anything else you run. No site list with greyed-out rows, no team page, no billing, no hint of the client next door. Which is exactly what you want if you're an agency.

There are two:

- **Guest viewer** — reads the dashboard for that site.
- **Guest editor** — reads it and can change that site's settings: goals, funnels, shields, path cleaning.

Neither can touch members, billing, other sites, or the team itself.

Invite one from the site's own sharing settings rather than the team members screen, since a guest belongs to a site. Same 48-hour expiry as any other invitation, same revoke button.

The design detail worth knowing: **guests deliberately sit outside the role ranking.** The five team roles are ordered — owner above admin above editor and so on — and guests are not on that ladder at all. So a permission check for "at least an editor" can never accidentally admit a guest editor from some unrelated site. It's a small thing that stops a whole category of quiet access bug.

Guests are unlimited, like team members. There's no seat cost to giving every client their own login.

**When you don't need a guest at all:** if somebody only ever looks and never signs in, a [shared link](/help/who-can-see-my-analytics/) is less work for both of you. Tokenized URL, optional password, optionally pinned to a segment the viewer can't remove, revocable in one click. That's usually the right answer for a monthly client report.

To move a whole site to another team — an agency handing a client their own account — there's a site transfer workflow that carries the analytics with it rather than orphaning them.
